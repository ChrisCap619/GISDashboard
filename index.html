Environmental Inequity GIS Portfolio

A spatial analysis of urban vegetation, income distribution, and environmental vulnerability across major California metropolitan regions using geospatial programming and interactive mapping.

Project Overview

This project investigates environmental inequity across San Francisco, San Diego, and Los Angeles by combining vegetation density, median household income, and a computed vulnerability index. The goal is to identify spatial disparities that may reflect unequal access to environmental resources.

Methodology

Data Sources: Census ZIP boundary shapefiles with income and vegetation attributes.

Processing: Data cleaned and reprojected to EPSG:4326 for web mapping compatibility.

Analysis: Vegetation ratio and income were normalized using percentile ranking.

Index Creation: Environmental inequity index calculated as combined inverse of vegetation and income ranks.

Classification: Natural Breaks (Jenks) used to categorize spatial patterns into 5 classes.
